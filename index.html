Product Managers are More Valuable and Less Protected Than Ever
Reforge (Anand Iyer & JZ event recap) · July 31, 2026
Key takeaway
The sharpest argument in PM writing this quarter: the entire product development process was built on engineering scarcity, and that assumption has collapsed — so two things ripple out. First, captaincy is no longer yours by default; it goes to whoever owns the hardest part of the project, which means the visibility that used to arrive automatically at mid-career now has to be earned. Second, when building is cheap, understanding becomes the only real constraint — “the spec is now more important than ever to the business.” The career implication is concrete and slightly uncomfortable: your leverage moves from controlling the roadmap to making strategy legible enough that other people build well without you. Anand’s counterweight is the line to internalize — “if you can make things twice as fast and you’re running in the wrong direction, it doesn’t matter.”

Nvidia’s Risky Business
Stratechery (Ben Thompson) · August 11, 2026
Key takeaway
Thompson traces the AI buildout’s funding escalator — free cash flow, then $194B of debt raised by four hyperscalers by early July, then Google’s $85B equity issuance, and now Nvidia partnering with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman and KKR to mobilize $500B of third-party capital, backstopped by Nvidia’s own residual-value guarantees. His historical parallel is Jay Cooke selling railroad bonds to retail investors in 1870, which ended in the Panic of 1873. The mental model shift for PMs: the cost of the intelligence in your product is a financing story, not just a pricing-page story, and it is currently subsidized by capital that expects returns on a schedule. Apply this by stress-testing any roadmap bet whose unit economics only work at today’s token prices.

I Tried the AI Model Built to Fix AI Writing
Every (Katie Parrott) · August 24, 2026
Key takeaway
Parrott tests a lab’s claim that the sameness of AI prose is a training problem, and finds the result less predictable but not actually better — her verdict is that model progress on writing at the major labs has stalled while coding got the attention. Her cover-band metaphor is the useful part: a band that knows every song but has one arrangement, so by the fourth track you stop absorbing the words. For any PM shipping AI-generated text into a product surface, internalize this as a ceiling rather than a bug awaiting a patch: fluency is solved, distinctiveness is not, and a fix is not obviously coming from the model layer. That makes house style, structure and human judgment the durable differentiators — including in your own writing, where the specs and strategy docs that read as unmistakably yours now stand out more, not less.

Applied AI Workflows for PMs

Must read
How I AI: How this PM uses Claude to handle 70% to 80% of his workday
Lenny’s Newsletter (Daniel Blum, PM at Melio) · August 31, 2026
Key takeaway
This is the most directly copyable PM workflow published in months. Blum built a self-improving assistant that manages his Notion board, scans Slack and email for context, and preps his week — but the three mechanics worth stealing are subtler. His morning brief flags what it doesn’t know, surfacing unfamiliar terms and projects and asking targeted questions to fill the gap. A weekly loop diffs Claude’s drafts against what he actually sent, learning from his instinctive edits rather than explicit feedback. And every skill logs friction so a weekly pass recommends its own updates — analytics for your internal tools. He’s candid that the first few weeks feel painfully slow; the compounding comes later. Apply this principle by picking one recurring output and building the draft-versus-sent diff loop for it this week.

Must read
More than just code review
Simon Willison · August 22, 2026
Key takeaway
Four sentences, one durable principle: the key skill for productive agent use is being able to confidently instruct and then confidently verify — and reviewing every line is only one way to verify, and never was the most effective one. The generalization to PM work is direct. Most people delegating to agents are strong on instruction and vague on verification, so they either rubber-stamp output or read all of it, and both fail at scale. The mental model shift: for every task you hand an agent, decide the verification method before you write the prompt — a spot-check, a known-answer test, a reconciliation against a second source. If you can’t name how you’ll check it, that task isn’t ready to delegate yet.

How I Polish Software That Agents Built
Every (Kieran Klaassen) · July 13, 2026
Key takeaway
Klaassen names the step nobody has a process for: opening your laptop to a stack of green pull requests that passed every automated check, and deciding whether any of it is actually good. His answer is a formal polish stage — run the app beside the agent, use it as a user would, and narrate what feels off until it doesn’t, like the email card that slid in from the top of the screen instead of opening from where he clicked. His framing is the part to keep: “a model doesn’t know what I would call good, sitting in front of this feature in this product for these users,” so without that judgment everything an agent produces is functional but forgettable. For any PM whose team is shipping agent-built work, apply this by making polish a named, owned step in the process rather than something that happens if someone happens to notice.
