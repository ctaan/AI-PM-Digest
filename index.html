Must read
After Automation
Every · Dan Shipper · May 21, 2026
Key takeaway
Dan Shipper's thesis: AI commoditizes "yesterday's competence" — anything explicit enough to train on — which collapses the value of default model output and creates demand for what's different, i.e. human expert judgment. His sharpest tool is the frame/framer distinction: benchmarks measure a model inside a frame someone chose, and every time a frame saturates, experts move to the edge and define the next one. The framing PMs should adopt: your enduring value is being the framer, bringing a live read on this customer, codebase, and moment that the training corpus can't. Apply it by using cheap execution to take on more ambitious, differentiated work rather than treating automation as headcount savings — when everyone can generate the same "decent start," the premium is on the specific, alive version only a human in the situation can produce.
