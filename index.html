Understanding ChatGPT Work
Simon Willison · August 30, 2026
Key takeaway
Willison does the work OpenAI didn’t: he reverse-engineers what ChatGPT Work actually does versus Chat, and the answer is a genuinely different product — code execution with open internet access, a full headless Chrome that can fill forms and run JavaScript against the DOM, a filesystem persisted across sessions, sub-agents, and deployable websites. His critique is the transferable lesson: OpenAI explains Work “in terms of what it’s for, not what it actually does,” and their official Chat-versus-Work guidance is useless because every category it lists is something people already do in Chat. That’s a positioning failure any PM can make. Apply this principle to your own feature docs — if a user can’t derive the decision rule from your description, you’ve written marketing, not documentation. Also note he flags Work as combining all three legs of his “lethal trifecta” (private data, untrusted content, exfiltration path), which is the right lens for reviewing any agentic feature on your own roadmap.

Must read
Product Management is Decidedly Not Dead: An Optimistic (and Realistic) Take
Reforge (Ravi Mehta event recap) · August 6, 2026
Key takeaway
The single most useful reframe here: most PM best practices were never designed to build the best products — they were designed to avoid expensive mistakes. Nobody ever wanted an MVP; MVPs exist because building was costly and you needed signal before spending more. The whole spec → wireframe → prototype → MVP ladder was a cost-justification structure, and when AI collapses the cost of working software the ladder collapses with it. What replaces the assembly line is closer to a jazz band, with working software as the artifact of communication rather than a PRD. The career implication is precise: your job moves from prioritizer to curator — from gatekeeping what gets built to judging what deserves to ship. Ravi’s companion point is the one to keep: things that move at human speed (customer conversations, stakeholder alignment, onboarding bandwidth, culture, brand) haven’t accelerated at all, so as the org speeds up, steering becomes the scarce skill. The craft-versus-taste framework for AI features is worth stealing outright.

Must read
How AI-native companies turn workflows into operating capability
OpenAI · September 1, 2026
Key takeaway
Three concrete workflows, and one of them is directly copyable this week: a GTM engineer at Clay gave every account a persistent workspace and a dedicated subagent that reviews primary sources overnight, then a coordinating agent turns all of it into a short list of priority moves each morning — saving about an hour of nightly triage, with evidence kept next to each recommendation so she can inspect sources before acting. Basis turned onboarding into a reusable skill by demonstrating it once and defining a trigger, known steps, tools and a clear definition of “done”; HR updates the skill when exceptions appear. The framing to adopt is their six-step design brief, especially “write the agent’s job description” — trigger, outcome, context, tools, permissions, required evidence, and where it must stop for human review. Also worth knowing for context: OpenAI’s Enterprise Signals shows top-decile firms now generating 8.3× the output tokens per user of typical firms, up from 2.6× in January.

Conceptual integrity and counting lines of code
Simon Willison · August 19, 2026
Key takeaway
Willison’s honest accounting of agent productivity: yes, agents let you produce a thousand lines of debugged code, and that is a real improvement — but only while quality holds, and holding it takes a large amount of skill, knowledge and experience. Volume is the easy part; conceptual integrity is the part that doesn’t come free. The mental model shift for PMs is to stop treating throughput as the metric that proves AI adoption is working. Output volume mostly shows that people are asking AI to do more, which is not the same as the work being better. For any PM reporting on an AI initiative, pair every throughput number with a quality or coherence measure, or you’ll end up defending a chart that means nothing.
