PM Thought Leaders

Must read
AI agents are your newest product feature. Is your security thinking keeping up?
Mind the Product (Mohit Bansal) · August 25, 2026
Key takeaway
The sharpest observation here: in agent discovery sessions, PMs are making security decisions without anyone naming them as such. Bansal isolates the four that matter — tool scope (what an adversary can hijack), trust levels (what the agent treats as instruction versus data), human-in-the-loop (a scoping decision keyed to reversibility, not a UX preference), and data access (least privilege decided at spec time, not implementation). His forcing question is one you can use verbatim this week: if this agent were acting for someone who wanted to cause damage instead of help, what could it do? The mental model shift is from “what can this feature do?” to “what can this feature be made to do?” — and his point about when to ask it is the hard part, because discovery is built for optimism. Two practices to steal outright: put the second question in your spec template so it gets asked every time, and before launch have someone hide an instruction inside content the agent reads to see whether it obeys. He also makes the career argument directly: agent features that can't clear enterprise security review don't ship to enterprise customers, so fluency in trust boundaries is a deal-closing skill, not a compliance chore.

Applied AI Workflows for PMs

Must read
From assistance to execution: How enterprises put AI to work
OpenAI · August 12, 2026
Key takeaway
Two studies across 10M+ enterprise messages, and the numbers reframe what “AI adoption” means. Frontier firms (top 10% of usage) now generate 8.3× the output tokens per active user of typical firms, up from 2.6× in January — and the gap tracks not with access to models but with adoption of the capabilities that connect agents to company context, tools and repeatable workflows. Concretely: 21% of weekly actives at frontier firms use Plugins and 19% use skills, versus 9% and 3% at typical firms; agentic use is spreading fastest outside engineering, with weekly Codex users up 108× in legal, 41× in sales, 41× in recruiting and 26× in marketing since February, against 5× in engineering. The finding most PMs will get wrong: usage is highest among early-career employees, not executives — six months post-adoption they sent 13 more messages per week than execs, which inverts the usual survey story. The framing to adopt: your leverage gap isn't a model gap, it's a workflow-packaging gap. This week, take one thing you do repeatedly and turn it into a shared skill with context and tools attached, rather than a prompt you retype.

More than just code review
Simon Willison · August 22, 2026
Key takeaway
Willison names the actual bottleneck skill for working with agents: confidently instructing them how to make a change, then confidently verifying the change landed correctly. His caveat is the useful part — sometimes that means reading every line, but eyeballing every line was never the most effective way to validate a change anyway, so the real work is choosing a verification method proportional to the risk. Apply this principle beyond code: every agent workflow you hand off needs a paired verification step you actually trust, and if you can't name how you'd check the output, you're not ready to delegate the task. That instruct-and-verify loop is the transferable competence, and it's the one worth practising deliberately.
