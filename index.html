Must read
How top PMs increase their leverage with AI
Lenny's Newsletter · Colin Matthews · June 30, 2026
Key takeaway
Colin Matthews (who has trained 30,000+ PMs) lays out a “three ladders of leverage” framework — personal, product, and systems — where each rung moves you from using AI to assist, to delegating whole tasks, to standing up self-checking systems. The concrete workflow to adopt this week: connect your LLM to the tools you already use (PostHog, Amplitude, Figma, Notion) via MCP, then delegate one complete to-do end-to-end — e.g. “run a retention analysis on this cohort and output an HTML doc, cite your sources” — and once it works, lock it in as a reusable skill. The mental model shift: the goal isn’t better prompting, it’s climbing from “AI helps me write” to “AI completes the task and I review,” picking the right rung for the work in front of you rather than maxing out every time.

Must read
The Folder Is the Agent
Every · Kieran Klaassen · April 13, 2026 (updated June 28)
Key takeaway
After three months of failing to make elaborate multi-agent “swarms” work, Kieran Klaassen found the real unit of leverage was mundane: a folder containing a CLAUDE.md/AGENT.md file, skill definitions, and accumulated context — a plain model “pointed at a folder” that turns it into a specialist. His pointed constraint: agents have no speed limit, but the human reviewing their output does, so orchestration complexity buys nothing if you can’t evaluate the results. The concrete practice to build this week: stop chasing agent frameworks and instead invest in one well-structured project folder — a context file that encodes how you name things, what you care about, and your standards — so any model you point at it inherits months of your judgment. Internalize this as the cheapest, most durable form of AI leverage available to a PM right now.

How agents are transforming work
OpenAI · June 25, 2026
Key takeaway
OpenAI’s economic-research paper documents agents changing the unit of knowledge work from short chatbot exchanges to delegated, long-horizon tasks: by May 2026, 80% of sampled users made at least one request estimated to exceed 30 minutes of human work, and non-developer adoption of Codex grew 137x since August 2025 — with Legal, Finance, and Recruiting flipping to agents as their primary AI tool. The mental model shift for PMs: the frontier isn’t “chat with AI” but “orchestrate multiple agents on hour-plus tasks,” and non-technical functions are crossing that line fast. Apply this by identifying the longest, most bounded chunk of your recurring work — a multi-step analysis or a full first-draft artifact — and handing it off as a single delegated task rather than a series of prompts, then building your review muscle around the output.
