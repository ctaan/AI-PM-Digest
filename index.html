Simon Willison · July 2, 2026
Key takeaway
Borrowing Geoffrey Litt’s “understand to participate” framing, Willison warns that as agents make larger, more sophisticated changes, you accrue “cognitive debt” if your understanding drifts from how the work actually functions — and once that happens, your ability to steer is meaningfully limited. For any PM directing AI, the principle to apply: don’t just accept agent output, stay fluent enough in the underlying work to interrogate and redirect it. Build the habit of having the agent explain its reasoning so you keep the conceptual grip that lets you participate rather than rubber-stamp.
