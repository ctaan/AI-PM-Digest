Who’s Afraid of Chinese Models?
Stratechery (Ben Thompson) · July 20, 2026
Key takeaway
Ben Thompson argues the panic over Kimi and Chinese open models is overblown: tokens are not a commodity but intelligence is, and today’s low prices are an artifact of a compute shortage, not a durable open-weight cost advantage. The sharpest reframe for PMs is his COGS-versus-R&D distinction — “free” weights still cost real money to serve, and a chattier model that burns more reasoning tokens can be more expensive per correct answer than a pricier one. Internalize this when you model AI feature economics: price on cost-per-resolved-task, not per-token or per-model sticker price, and remember that the integrated model-plus-harness layer is where durable differentiation and margin actually live.

Understand to participate
Simon Willison · July 2, 2026
Key takeaway
Riffing on a Geoffrey Litt talk, Willison names the risk of “cognitive debt” — as agents make larger and larger changes, your understanding drifts from how the work actually functions, and you lose the fluency needed to keep steering. The career implication for PMs delegating heavily to AI: keep enough hands-on understanding of your product, data, and decisions to stay an active participant, not just an approver of outputs you can no longer interrogate. Apply this by periodically doing a task yourself that you’ve been fully delegating, as a deliberate check against your own understanding decaying.
